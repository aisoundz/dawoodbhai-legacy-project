Dealing with grief after the death of your baby

There is no right way to grieve a baby. These pages offer gentle guidance for parents, partners, and the people who love them.

On this page

Understanding grief

Grief is the deep sadness that follows the loss of someone you love. When a baby dies, parents grieve not only the child but also the future they imagined together. Grief can come in waves and can return on birthdays, due dates, and holidays.

Feelings parents often describe include:

Shock or numbnessDeep sadnessAngerGuiltFeeling aloneTrouble sleepingLoss of appetiteAching arms

All of these are normal. Grief has no schedule, and there is no deadline for feeling better.

How partners grieve

Two people who lost the same baby may grieve in very different ways. One may want to talk about it often. The other may grieve quietly or throw themselves into work. Different does not mean one person cares less.

  • Tell each other what you need, even if it changes day to day.
  • Make room for each other's way of grieving.
  • Consider seeing a counselor together if you feel distance growing.

Talking with children

Brothers and sisters feel the loss too, even when they are very young. Use simple, honest words such as "the baby died" rather than "we lost the baby" or "the baby went to sleep," which can confuse or frighten children.

  • Let them ask questions, and answer as honestly as you can for their age.
  • Reassure them that nothing they did or thought caused the death.
  • Invite them to help remember the baby with a drawing, a letter, or a special object.

Where to find support

  • Your healthcare provider can answer medical questions about what happened.
  • A hospital social worker can help with medical bills, insurance, and funeral arrangements.
  • A grief counselor or therapist can help you work through your feelings over time.
  • A religious or spiritual leader can offer comfort and help with rituals and services.
  • Support groups for parents who have lost a baby, in person or online. See our partner programs.

Taking care of yourself

Your body is healing too, while your heart is grieving.

  • Go to your postpartum or follow-up checkup, and ask any questions you have.
  • Eat regular meals, drink water, and rest when you can.
  • Avoid alcohol, limit caffeine, and stay away from smoking.
  • Accept help with meals, errands, and childcare.
  • If deep sadness, hopelessness, or loss of interest lasts more than two weeks, talk to your provider. These can be signs of depression, which is treatable.

Postpartum Support International offers free help for grieving and postpartum parents: postpartum.net

Ways to remember

Many families find comfort in honoring their baby in lasting ways.

A keepsake or memory boxA memorial or naming serviceJournaling or writing lettersMarking their birthday each yearPlanting a tree or gardenMemorial jewelryA star in our Remembrance Sky

Coping with death and loss

In the weeks and months ahead, the world may expect you to move on before you are ready. Be patient with yourself.

  • Put off big decisions, like moving or changing jobs, when you can.
  • Plan ahead for hard days such as due dates and holidays, and decide how you want to spend them.
  • It is okay to say your baby's name and to ask others to say it too.
  • Joy and grief can live side by side. Feeling good for a moment is not forgetting.

Coping with trauma

A sudden emergency, a frightening delivery, or time in the NICU can leave lasting effects. You might replay events, feel on edge, avoid reminders like the hospital, or have nightmares.

  • These are common reactions to a traumatic experience, not a sign of weakness.
  • Grounding techniques, such as slow breathing and naming what you can see and hear, can help in hard moments.
  • A therapist trained in trauma or perinatal loss can help. Ask your provider or a hospital social worker for a referral.
If you are in crisis

If you are thinking about suicide or harming yourself, call or text 988 to reach the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline at any hour. If you are in immediate danger, call 911.
